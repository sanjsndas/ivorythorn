About the Circle Rooted in observation, woven by hand — the Leafhaven story

⟡ A quiet journal for the deep gardener

Ivorythorn · Leafhaven began as a field notebook under an old oak, and grew into a living archive of soil, silence, and reciprocal care. We are a small collective of gardeners, writers, and ecologists who believe that gardening is not a hobby but a conversation — with the land, with the seasons, with the unseen mycelial networks that hold the world together.

Our roots

Founded in 2020, the Leafhaven Circle emerged from a shared frustration with conventional gardening advice: too much product, too little patience. We started documenting our experiments in polyculture, no-till methods, and heirloom preservation. What began as a private log became a public journal — and eventually, this website.

Today, we host workshops, maintain a seed library, and publish field notes that blend science, folklore, and practical wisdom. Our contributors include botanists, permaculture designers, and poets who garden by moonlight.

“The garden is not a plot of land; it is a plot of time. We are merely its stewards for a season.”
— H. Marlow, founding editor

What we believe

  • Biodiversity over uniformity: every garden is a refuge for species, great and small.
  • Soil first: healthy soil grows healthy plants, communities, and minds.
  • Slow knowledge: the best insights come from years, not hours, of observation.
  • Open source: we share our findings freely, because the earth belongs to no one.

Frequently asked questions

Is the Circle open to new members?

Yes. We welcome gardeners of all levels. Join our monthly field calls or contribute to the seed library.

Do you offer online courses?

We host seasonal webinars and a self-paced rhizome method course. Check our journal for updates.

How can I support the Circle?

Share our work, plant a guild, or donate seeds to our exchange. Every action roots us deeper.
